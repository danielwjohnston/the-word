# Project Brief, Version 2 (governing document)

*This brief supersedes the original prompt wherever they differ. It incorporates the author's directions of 7 November 2026: "No length trim necessary. Commentaries are useful; find suitable replacements. The original manuscripts always have the priority. No translation is more important than another. Where useful draw from many: Message, Amplified, ESV, NASB, NLT, WEB, BSB and any others. Revealing God's word is the point. If a text is mentioned or alluded to within also highlight that text. If anything needs permission from someone or copyright abandon and scrub it. God's word should be fully free and open and copyleft."*

## 1. Purpose

**Reveal God's word.** *Jude, Unfolded: An Exploration of Every Verse, Word, Image, and Meaning* is a book-length expository commentary on the Epistle of Jude for thoughtful Christian readers without academic training. Every editorial choice serves one end: helping readers see what the text says, what it meant, and how it may be lived. Length is governed by substance, so no length trimming is required.

## 2. The freedom rule (overrides everything else)

1. **Everything in the book must be free and open.** Material is admissible only if it is in the public domain, dedicated to the public domain (CC0), or released under an open license compatible with copyleft redistribution (CC BY or CC BY-SA). The book itself is released under **CC BY-SA 4.0**.
2. **Scrub anything that needs permission.** If a text, translation, commentary, image, or dataset requires anyone's permission to quote or redistribute, or is restricted by copyright in any jurisdiction, it is removed. This applies even when its owner offers limited "gratis use" terms. Its readings and ideas are not reproduced, and it is not relied on as an authority.
3. **Consequences for requested translations.** The Message, the Amplified Bible, the ESV, the NASB, and the NLT all require permission and are under copyright, so they are excluded. Their *roles* are filled by free equivalents:

   | Requested (excluded) | Role | Free replacement(s) used |
   |---|---|---|
   | The Message | paraphrase in contemporary idiom | Translation for Translators (T4T, CC BY-SA), Free Bible Version (FBV, CC BY-SA), Open English Bible (OEB, PD) |
   | Amplified Bible | expanded rendering that makes implied meaning explicit | T4T (which states implied information explicitly) |
   | ESV / NASB | formal-equivalence modern English | Literal Standard Version (LSV, CC BY-SA), Unlocked Literal Bible (ULB, CC BY-SA), American Standard Version (ASV, PD), Revised Version (RV, PD), Young's Literal Translation (YLT, PD) |
   | NLT | readable modern English | Berean Standard Bible (BSB, PD), World English Bible (WEB, PD), OEB (PD) |

   Also excluded: NIV, NET, NKJV, CSB, NRSV, GOD'S WORD, ERV (Easy-to-Read), EMTV (licensed non-commercial and no-derivatives), the Bible in Basic English (public domain only in the United States), and the King James Version (under Crown letters patent in the United Kingdom). Webster's 1833 revision and the 1599 Geneva Bible represent the KJV tradition freely.
4. **No unverifiable or restricted commentaries.** Modern copyrighted commentaries are replaced by public-domain commentaries and ancient sources that were actually read (§5).

## 3. Priority of the original text

1. **The original manuscripts always take priority.** Each passage is presented first in Greek: the SBL Greek New Testament (CC BY 4.0), with its apparatus. It is then checked against the readings of the earliest manuscripts themselves, using transcriptions from the INTF New Testament Virtual Manuscript Room (CC BY 4.0) for P72, P78, Sinaiticus (01), Alexandrinus (02), Vaticanus (03), Ephraemi (04), Ψ (044), 1739, K (018), and L (020), and the Robinson–Pierpont Byzantine text (PD).
2. **The Old Testament is quoted in Hebrew** (Westminster Leningrad Codex, PD) and, where Jude's wording depends on it, in the Greek Septuagint (Brenton's edition, PD).
3. **Interpretation rests on the original wording.** Translations are witnesses to the meaning of the original, never its substitute.

## 4. Parity of translations

1. **No translation is more important than another.** No English version is "primary." Every passage of Jude is shown in **eighteen free translations**, listed in approximately chronological order, not in order of preference: Tyndale, Geneva, Douay-Rheims, Webster, Noyes, Revised Version, Darby, Young's Literal, ASV, WEB, OEB, T4T, BSB, ULB, FBV, LSV, Majority Standard Bible, and the Text-Critical English NT.
2. **Every English quotation is labeled with its translation.** Running commentary may quote whichever free translation most clearly shows the point under discussion.
3. **Headings use the original language.** Each phrase heading in the commentary gives the Greek phrase first, with a literal gloss made for this book.

## 5. Commentaries and ancient sources

1. **Use public-domain commentaries** and cite the specific passage read. Examples: Calvin; Luther (Gillett's 1859 translation); Matthew Henry; Poole; Gill; Bengel; Benson; Barnes; Jamieson–Fausset–Brown; Huther in Meyer's Commentary; Plumptre in the Cambridge Bible; Plummer in Ellicott's Commentary and in the Expositor's Bible; the Pulpit Commentary; Vincent's *Word Studies*; Mayor in the Expositor's Greek Testament; and the 1599 Geneva notes.
2. **Use ancient and patristic sources in public-domain translations**, read directly: 1 Enoch (Charles, 1917); the Targums (Etheridge); Josephus (Whiston); Philo (Yonge); the Testaments of the Twelve Patriarchs; Clement, Origen, Tertullian, Irenaeus, Eusebius, Jerome, Augustine, and Athanasius (ANF/NPNF); the *Didache*; Pliny; Theophrastus.
3. **Use public-domain lexical tools**: Abbott-Smith (1922), with Mayor and Moulton–Milligan cited through it.
4. **Keep every claim traceable to a source that was actually read.** Where no free source supports a claim, the book presents it as the author's own reasoning from the primary evidence, or drops it.

## 6. Highlight every text mentioned or alluded to

1. **Show every cited text in full.** Every biblical, apocryphal, or 1 Enoch text cited or alluded to in a chapter is displayed in full in that chapter's final section, **"L. Texts Highlighted in This Chapter."** Biblical texts appear in the original language (Hebrew, Greek, or LXX Greek) together with two free English translations. For 1 Enoch, which survives complete only in Ge'ez, the panel gives Charles's English.
2. **Link references to their panel.** In the assembled book, references in the text link to their panel entry.
3. **Quote passages discussed in the prose.** Texts that a chapter discusses at length are also quoted inline where they are discussed.

## 7. Chapter structure (unchanged in substance)

Each main chapter has these sections:

- **A.** The Text (Greek, manuscript evidence, eighteen translations)
- **B.** Plain-English Orientation
- **C.** Phrase-by-Phrase Exploration (Greek-led headings)
- **D.** Original-Language Investigation
- **E.** Historical and Cultural World
- **F.** Connections Across Scripture
- **G.** Scholarly and Theological Questions
- **H.** The Passage Within Jude
- **I.** Contemporary Understanding (clearly separated from the original meaning)
- **J.** Reflection and Discussion
- **K.** Sources
- **L.** Texts Highlighted in This Chapter

Deep dives stay where the subject arises.

## 8. Integrity rules (carried forward)

- **Labels.** Separate textual fact, historical evidence, consensus, competing interpretations, tradition, inference, and application.
- **Fabrication.** Never invent references, readings, definitions, or quotations.
- **Fairness.** Present competing views fairly, and avoid denominational polemics.
- **Etymology.** Do not let etymology dictate meaning.
- **Verification.** Automated audits must pass with zero issues before release:
  - quotation fidelity for every labeled translation;
  - coverage of every verse and key phrase;
  - footnotes;
  - chapter structure;
  - Greek spelling;
  - a check that no excluded translation is quoted.

## 9. Deliverables

`manuscript.md` (the editable master); exports in EPUB, DOCX, PDF, and HTML; the research ledger, coverage matrix, interpretive decisions, data notes, verified texts (Greek, manuscripts, free translations), bibliography (free sources only), quality review, and progress log; and tools that rebuild and re-audit everything from openly licensed sources.
