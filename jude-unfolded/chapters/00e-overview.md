# The Whole Letter at a Glance {.unnumbered}

Jude is short enough to read in one sitting, and it should be read that way before it is taken apart. Its first hearers would have heard it read aloud as a whole, probably at a gathering of the church. Read it now, slowly, ideally aloud.

## The Epistle of Jude in the Literal Standard Version

> **1** Judas, a servant of Jesus Christ, and brother of James, to those called, having been loved in God [the] Father, and having been kept in Jesus Christ, **2** mercy and peace and love be multiplied to you!
>
> **3** Beloved, using all diligence to write to you concerning our common salvation, I had necessity to write to you, exhorting [you] to fight for the faith once delivered to the holy ones, **4** for there came in certain men stealthily, having been written beforehand to this judgment long ago, impious, perverting the grace of our God to licentiousness, and denying our only Master and Lord—Jesus Christ.
>
> **5** Now I intend to remind you, you once having known all this, that Jesus, having saved a people out of the land of Egypt, He secondly destroyed those not having believed; **6** messengers also, those who did not keep their own principality, but left their proper dwelling, He has kept in eternal bonds under darkness until [the] judgment of [the] great day, **7** as Sodom and Gomorrah, and the cities around them, in like manner to these [messengers], having given themselves to whoredom, and having gone after other flesh, have been set before [as] an example, undergoing the justice of continuous fire.
>
> **8** In like manner, nevertheless, those dreaming indeed defile the flesh, and they disregard lordship, and they speak evil of glorious [ones], **9** yet Michael, the chief messenger, when disputing with the Devil, reasoning about the body of Moses, did not dare to bring against [him] a judgment of slander, but said, “The LORD rebuke you!” **10** But these, indeed, whatever [things] they have not known, they speak evil of; and whatever [they know], like the irrational beasts, they understand naturally—in these they are corrupted; **11** woe to them! Because they went on in the way of Cain, and they rushed to the deceit of Balaam for reward, and in the controversy of Korah they perished.
>
> **12** These are stains at your love-feasts, feasting together with you without fear, shepherding themselves; waterless clouds, being carried away by winds; autumnal trees without fruit, having died twice, having been uprooted; **13** wild waves of a sea, foaming up their own shameful [deeds]; stars going astray, for whom the gloom of the darkness throughout the age has been kept.
>
> **14** And Enoch, the seventh from Adam, also prophesied to these, saying, “Behold, the LORD has come with myriads of His holy ones, **15** to do judgment against all, and to convict all their impious ones, concerning all their works of impiety that they did impiously, and concerning all the harsh [words] that impious sinners spoke against Him.” **16** These are discontented grumblers, walking according to their own lusts; and their mouth speaks swollen words, giving admiration to persons for the sake of profit;
>
> **17** but you, beloved, remember the sayings spoken before by the apostles of our Lord Jesus Christ, **18** that they said to you, that in the last time there will be scoffers, following after their own lusts of impieties, **19** these are those causing divisions, natural men, not having the Spirit.
>
> **20** But you, beloved, building yourselves up on your most holy faith, praying in the Holy Spirit, **21** keep yourselves in the love of God, waiting for the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ [resulting] in continuous life; **22** and, indeed, show mercy to those who are doubting, **23** and rescue others by snatching [them] out of fire; but show mercy to others in fear, hating even the coat having been stained from the flesh.
>
> **24** And to Him who is able to guard you without stumbling, and to set [you] in the presence of His glory unblemished, in gladness, **25** to the only wise God our Savior, [is] glory and greatness, power and authority, both now and forever! Amen.
>
> — Jude 1–25, LSV

## The shape of the letter

Read as a whole, Jude has a clear architecture: a bright frame around a dark center.

| Section | Verses | Content | Key words |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Greeting** | 1–2 | Sender; recipients called, loved, kept; wish for mercy, peace, love | *kept*, *love*, *mercy* |
| **Occasion and appeal** | 3–4 | Jude meant to write about salvation, but must urge readers to contend for the faith, because intruders have slipped in | *beloved*, *faith*, *impious* |
| **The case against the intruders** | 5–19 | Five "texts," each followed by "these" applying it to the intruders | *these*, *impious*, *judgment*, *kept* |
| — first text | 5–7 | Three examples of judgment: wilderness generation, angels, Sodom | |
| — application | 8 | "those dreaming…" | |
| — second text | 9 | Michael's restraint in the dispute over Moses' body | |
| — application | 10–13 | "these … speak evil"; woe (Cain, Balaam, Korah); images from nature | |
| — third text | 14–15 | Enoch's prophecy of the Lord's coming with holy myriads | |
| — application | 16 | "These are … grumblers" | |
| — fourth text | 17–18 | The apostles' prediction of scoffers | |
| — application | 19 | "these are those causing divisions" | |
| **Exhortation** | 20–23 | Build, pray, keep yourselves, wait; show mercy, rescue, show mercy with fear | *beloved*, *keep*, *love*, *mercy* |
| **Doxology** | 24–25 | Praise to the God who is able to guard and present his people | *guard*, *glory* |

(Some outlines count the woe of v. 11 as a separate "text" and vv. 12–13 as its application, giving five texts in all. The structure is the same either way.)

Three features of this design are worth carrying into every chapter.

### 1. The frame interprets the center

The darkest material in the letter, judgment, darkness, and fire, sits between a greeting that calls the readers loved and kept and a doxology that praises God for keeping them from stumbling. The structure itself makes a theological claim: the readers' security does not depend on the absence of danger. The danger is real, but the one who keeps them is greater. A reading of Jude that remembers only the middle has misread the letter.

### 2. "Keep" runs through the whole

The Greek verb τηρέω (*tēreō*, "keep, guard, preserve") appears five times, and its near relative φυλάσσω (*phylassō*, "guard") once more at the climax:

| Verse | LSV | Who keeps what |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "having been kept in Jesus Christ" | God keeps the believers |
| 6 | "those who did not keep their own principality" | Angels failed to keep their position |
| 6 | "He has kept in eternal bonds" | God keeps the rebel angels for judgment |
| 13 | "the gloom of the darkness … has been kept" | Darkness is kept for the wanderers |
| 21 | "keep yourselves in the love of God" | Believers are to keep themselves |
| 24 | "Him who is able to guard you" | God guards the believers |

Two kinds of keeping run through the letter: protective keeping for those who belong to God, and custodial keeping for those who rebel. The angels "did not keep" what was theirs, and so they are "kept" for judgment. The readers are "kept" by God, and so they are to "keep" themselves in his love. The whole letter turns on this wordplay.

### 3. "Beloved" vs. "these"

Jude addresses his readers as "beloved" (ἀγαπητοί, *agapētoi*) three times (vv. 3, 17, 20). Each time the address marks a turning point toward them. He refers to the intruders as "these" (οὗτοι, *houtoi*) five times (vv. 8, 10, 12, 16, 19), each time applying an ancient example to them. The letter moves between the two groups, and its rhetoric depends on the contrast. In vv. 22–23, however, the line between them becomes porous. Some who have been influenced by the intruders can still be shown mercy and snatched from the fire. Jude's contrast exists to protect people, not to write them off.

## Reading strategy for the chapters ahead

As you work through the commentary, it may help to keep three questions in mind for each passage:

1. **What is Jude asking his readers to *see*?** Much of the letter is an argument from examples: "remember," "behold," "these are."
2. **What is Jude asking his readers to *do*?** The commands are few but weighty: contend (v. 3), remember (v. 17), build, pray, keep, wait (vv. 20–21), show mercy, rescue (vv. 22–23).
3. **What is Jude asking his readers to *trust*?** The letter begins and ends with God's keeping. Everything between is written by someone who believes God is able.
