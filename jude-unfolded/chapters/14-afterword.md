# Afterword: Reading Jude Whole {.unnumbered}

We have walked through Jude phrase by phrase, stopping at reefs and clouds, angels and archangels, Enoch and the love feast. It is worth stepping back, as Jude's first hearers would have done when the reading ended, and hearing the letter once more as a single word.

Jude wrote to people he loved about something he loved, "our common salvation." What he found was a community in danger. People had slipped in who spoke impressively, claimed visions, sat at the common table, and taught that grace made holiness unnecessary. Jude did not respond with a treatise. He responded with memory. Remember the people saved from Egypt who did not believe. Remember the angels who left their place and Sodom's fire. Remember Michael, who did not presume to judge even the Devil. Remember Cain, Balaam, and Korah. Remember what Enoch said and what the apostles told you. In each memory, Jude saw the present more clearly. The story of God's people had seen this danger before, and God had not been defeated by it.

Yet the letter does not end in memory, and it certainly does not end in denunciation. It ends with a community building itself up on a most holy faith, praying in the Holy Spirit, keeping itself in the love of God, and waiting for the mercy of Jesus Christ. It ends with that community reaching out in mercy to the wavering, snatching the endangered from the fire, and showing compassion even where caution is needed. And it ends with praise to the God who is able to keep his people from stumbling and to bring them, faultless and rejoicing, into the presence of his glory.

Three sentences may summarize what Jude asks of every generation that reads him:

**Remember what God has done and said.** The faith was delivered once for all. Its shape is known. Novelty is not the measure of truth.

**Keep yourselves in the love of God.** Faithfulness is not mainly a matter of fighting enemies but of remaining where God's love has placed us, through building up one another, prayer, and hope.

**Show mercy.** Those who contend most earnestly for the faith should be the quickest to reach out to the doubting and the endangered, because they themselves live by mercy.

Around these three sentences stand the two great statements that frame the letter: you have been called, loved, and kept; and God is able to guard you without stumbling. Between them lies everything else, the danger, the warnings, and the work. Jude's readers, then and now, are invited to live in the space those two statements define, in confidence that the one who kept them from the beginning will keep them to the end.

To Him be glory. Amen.
