# Appendix A. Significant Textual Variants in Jude {.unnumbered}

This appendix collects the variation units in Jude that affect translation or interpretation. The manuscripts come first: section A of each chapter shows, verse by verse, what ten of the earliest and most important Greek manuscripts read, checked against the transcriptions of the INTF New Testament Virtual Manuscript Room (CC BY 4.0). This appendix draws those readings together. For each unit it also gives the reading of the SBL Greek New Testament (SBLGNT), the Nestle–Aland 28th edition (NA28), the Robinson–Pierpont Byzantine text (RP), and the Textus Receptus (TR), and it shows how each of the eighteen free English translations reads it.[^aa-1] Approximate manuscript percentages, where given, come from the Text-Critical Greek New Testament notes. They count manuscripts without weighing them and are included only to show how a reading is distributed.

**How to read the tables.** "+" means "adds," and "–" means "omits." "Byz" refers to the Byzantine majority text.

## A.3 Notes on the major units, with the manuscript evidence

**Jude 1: "loved" or "sanctified."** ἠγαπημένοις, "loved," is the reading of P72, ℵ, A, B, Ψ, and 1739; ἡγιασμένοις, "sanctified," is the reading of K and L and of about 90% of all manuscripts. The critical editions all read "loved," judging it the earlier and harder reading. "Sanctified in God the Father" looks like a scribal improvement of the unusual "loved in God the Father," perhaps influenced by 1 Cor 1:2. See Chapter 1.

**Jude 4: "only Master [God] and Lord."** The added θεόν, "God," is absent from P72, P78, ℵ, A, B, C, and 1739, and present in the later manuscripts Ψ, K, and L. It appears in the Byzantine tradition and produced the traditional English rendering "denying the only Lord God, and our Lord Jesus Christ" (WBS). Since a single article governs the nouns, even the longer text most naturally refers all the titles to Jesus, as the WEB's rendering of the Majority Text shows: "our only Master, God, and Lord, Jesus Christ." See Chapter 2.

**Jude 5: "Jesus" or "the Lord."** This is the most discussed variant in the letter. "Jesus" (written ΙΣ) is read by A, B, and 1739. "The Lord" (ΚΣ) is read by ℵ, the first hand of C, Ψ, K, and L, and by most later manuscripts; a corrector of C changed it to "God." The early papyrus P72 has "God Christ." The editors of NA28 adopted "Jesus," reversing NA27, and the SBLGNT reads it too. The placement of "once" also varies. See the deep dive in Chapter 3.

**Jude 15: "all the impious" or "every soul."** "Every soul" (πᾶσαν ψυχήν), the reading of NA28, is found in P72 and ℵ, and corresponds to 1 Enoch 1:9's "all flesh" (Charles). "All the impious" (πάντας τοὺς ἀσεβεῖς), the reading of SBLGNT, is found in A, B, C, and Ψ, and matches Jude's emphasis on "impious"; 1739 has "all impious people." K and L add "their," as the Byzantine text does. See Chapter 8.

**Jude 19: "who cause divisions" or "who separate themselves."** The TR's added ἑαυτούς creates the traditional English rendering "These are they who separate themselves" (WBS), suggesting withdrawal. Among the manuscripts checked, only C and a corrector of 1739 have it. The critical and Byzantine texts lack it, which gives "these are the ones who cause divisions." See Chapter 10.

**Jude 22–23: two groups or three?** The manuscripts preserve at least six forms. ℵ and Ψ have three clauses (mercy, rescue, mercy with fear), as do the SBLGNT and NA28. A and 1739 also have three clauses but begin with "refute" (ἐλέγχετε) instead of "show mercy," as do Tregelles and the Tyndale House Greek New Testament (as reported). B has two clauses, "have mercy on some who doubt, saving them by snatching them from fire; have mercy on others with fear," the form printed by Westcott and Hort. C has two clauses beginning with "refute." K and L have the two-clause Byzantine form, and P72 has a short two-clause form of its own ("snatch some from fire; on those who dispute have mercy with fear"). Clement of Alexandria's paraphrase suggests a two-part text. See the deep dive in Chapter 12.

**Jude 25: the shape of the doxology.** "Through Jesus Christ our Lord" is present in every manuscript checked except K. "Before all time" is present in ℵ, A, B, C, Ψ, L, and 1739 (1739 without the article) and absent from P72 and K. Both phrases are absent from the Byzantine text and the TR, and so from the LSV and the other translations that follow them (see A.2). "Wise," found here in K and L, is probably borrowed from Rom 16:27. See Chapter 13.

## A.4 What the variants show about the LSV

The LSV states in its preface that its New Testament is based on the Textus Receptus and the Majority Text, departing from them "in certain, specific instances … where the evidence seems incontrovertible."[^aa-2] Jude illustrates this policy well. In vv. 1, 3, 4, 5, and 22–23 the LSV adopts readings of the critical editions against the Byzantine majority. In vv. 15 and 25 it keeps the Byzantine readings. Readers of this book should therefore keep in mind that the LSV's English does not always correspond to a single printed Greek edition. The commentary notes every place where that matters.

## A.5 How significant are these variants?

None of Jude's variants affects any central Christian doctrine. Several affect how particular verses are read: whether Jesus is named as Israel's deliverer (v. 5), how many groups the readers are to help (vv. 22–23), and whether glory is ascribed to God "through Jesus Christ" and ‘before all time’ (v. 25). Honest reading includes knowing where the text is uncertain, and good textual scholarship lets readers see how much of the text is secure. For Jude, by any measure, almost all of it is.
