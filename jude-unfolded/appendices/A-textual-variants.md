# Appendix A. Significant Textual Variants in Jude {.unnumbered}

This appendix collects the variation units in Jude that affect translation or interpretation. For each unit it gives the reading of the SBL Greek New Testament (SBLGNT), the Nestle–Aland 28th edition (NA28), the Robinson–Pierpont Byzantine text (RP), and the Textus Receptus (TR), and it indicates which reading the LSV follows. The data come from the SBLGNT apparatus, the updated apparatus citing NA28 in the eBible.org SBLGNT edition, and the Robinson–Pierpont and TR texts themselves.[^aa-1] Approximate manuscript percentages, where given, come from the Text-Critical Greek New Testament notes. They count manuscripts without weighing them and are included only to show how a reading is distributed.

**How to read the tables.** "+" means "adds," and "–" means "omits." "Byz" refers to the Byzantine majority text.

## A.2 Notes on the major units

**Jude 1: "loved" or "sanctified."** About 90% of manuscripts read "sanctified." The critical editions all read "loved," judging it the earlier and harder reading. "Sanctified in God the Father" looks like a scribal improvement of the unusual "loved in God the Father," perhaps influenced by 1 Cor 1:2. See Chapter 1.

**Jude 4: "only Master [God] and Lord."** The added θεόν appears in the Byzantine tradition and produced the KJV's "denying the only Lord God, and our Lord Jesus Christ." Since a single article governs the nouns, even the longer text most naturally refers all the titles to Jesus, as the WEB's rendering of the Majority Text shows: "our only Master, God, and Lord, Jesus Christ." See Chapter 2.

**Jude 5: "Jesus" or "the Lord."** This is the most discussed variant in the letter. "Jesus" is read by important early manuscripts, including Codex Alexandrinus and Codex Vaticanus. The early papyrus P72 has "God Christ." Most later manuscripts have "the Lord." The editors of NA28 adopted "Jesus," reversing NA27, and the SBLGNT reads it too. The placement of "once" also varies. See the deep dive in Chapter 3.

**Jude 15: "all the impious" or "every soul."** NA28's "every soul" corresponds to 1 Enoch 1:9's "all flesh" (Charles). SBLGNT's "all the impious" matches Jude's emphasis on "impious." The Byzantine text adds "their." See Chapter 8.

**Jude 19: "who cause divisions" or "who separate themselves."** The TR's added ἑαυτούς creates the KJV's "These be they who separate themselves," suggesting withdrawal. The critical and Byzantine texts lack it, which gives "these are the ones who cause divisions." See Chapter 10.

**Jude 22–23: two groups or three?** Five main forms survive. The SBLGNT, NA28, and LSV have three clauses (mercy, rescue, mercy with fear). Westcott–Hort, the Byzantine text, and P72 have two clauses in different arrangements. Tregelles and the Tyndale House Greek New Testament (as reported) read "refute" (ἐλέγχετε) instead of "show mercy" in the first clause. Clement of Alexandria's paraphrase suggests a two-part text. See the deep dive in Chapter 12.

**Jude 25: the shape of the doxology.** The critical text's "through Jesus Christ our Lord" and "before all time" are absent from the Byzantine text and the LSV. The Byzantine "wise" is probably borrowed from Rom 16:27. See Chapter 13.

## A.3 What the variants show about the LSV

The LSV states in its preface that its New Testament is based on the Textus Receptus and the Majority Text, departing from them "in certain, specific instances … where the evidence seems incontrovertible."[^aa-2] Jude illustrates this policy well. In vv. 1, 3, 4, 5, and 22–23 the LSV adopts readings of the critical editions against the Byzantine majority. In vv. 15 and 25 it keeps the Byzantine readings. Readers of this book should therefore keep in mind that the LSV's English does not always correspond to a single printed Greek edition. The commentary notes every place where that matters.

## A.4 How significant are these variants?

None of Jude's variants affects any central Christian doctrine. Several affect how particular verses are read: whether Jesus is named as Israel's deliverer (v. 5), how many groups the readers are to help (vv. 22–23), and whether glory is ascribed to God "through Jesus Christ" and ‘before all time’ (v. 25). Honest reading includes knowing where the text is uncertain, and good textual scholarship lets readers see how much of the text is secure. For Jude, by any measure, almost all of it is.
