# Glossary of Greek and Theological Terms {.unnumbered}

Greek words are listed by transliteration, with the Greek form and the verse in Jude where it appears. Lexical summaries draw on Abbott-Smith's *Manual Greek Lexicon of the New Testament* (1922) unless noted.

## Greek words

**agapē** (ἀγάπη), vv. 2, 12, 21. "Love." In the plural (v. 12), "love feasts," the community's shared meals. See Chapter 6.

**agapētoi** (ἀγαπητοί), vv. 3, 17, 20. "Beloved," Jude's address to his readers.

**agalliasis** (ἀγαλλίασις), v. 24. "Exultation, exuberant joy."

**akarpos** (ἄκαρπος), v. 12. "Fruitless."

**alogos** (ἄλογος), v. 10. "Without reason"; τὰ ἄλογα ζῷα, "irrational animals."

**amōmos** (ἄμωμος), v. 24. "Without blemish," used of sacrificial animals, and morally "faultless."

**antilogia** (ἀντιλογία), v. 11. "Contradiction, dispute, rebellion." In the LXX it is used for the "water of contention" (Meribah).

**anydros** (ἄνυδρος), v. 12. "Waterless."

**aphobōs** (ἀφόβως), v. 12. "Without fear," here meaning without reverence.

**apodiorizō** (ἀποδιορίζω), v. 19. "To mark off; to make divisions." NT hapax.

**aptaistos** (ἄπταιστος), v. 24. "Without stumbling, sure-footed." NT hapax.

**archangelos** (ἀρχάγγελος), v. 9. "Archangel, chief messenger" (LSV). Used of Michael.

**archē** (ἀρχή), v. 6. "Beginning; rule, domain." The angels' assigned position (LSV "principality").

**asebēs, asebeia, asebeō** (ἀσεβής, ἀσέβεια, ἀσεβέω), vv. 4, 15, 18. "Impious/ungodly," "impiety," "to act impiously." Jude's key description of the intruders.

**aselgeia** (ἀσέλγεια), v. 4. "Licentiousness, shameless excess."

**atheteō** (ἀθετέω), v. 8. "To set aside, reject."

**aidios** (ἀΐδιος), v. 6. "Everlasting."

**aiōn, aiōnios** (αἰών, αἰώνιος), vv. 7, 13, 21, 25. "Age" and "age-long, eternal." The LSV renders αἰώνιος "continuous."

**blasphēmeō, blasphēmia** (βλασφημέω, βλασφημία), vv. 8–10. "To slander, revile"; "slander." Used of abusive speech against anyone, not only against God.

**deigma** (δεῖγμα), v. 7. "A specimen, example (as a warning)." NT hapax.

**despotēs** (δεσπότης), v. 4. "Master, owner, sovereign." Used of Jesus.

**diabolos** (διάβολος), v. 9. "Slanderer, accuser; the Devil."

**diakrinomai** (διακρίνομαι), vv. 9, 22. ‘To dispute’ (v. 9) or ‘to doubt, waver’ (v. 22).

**dikē** (δίκη), v. 7. "Justice; the penalty carried out."

**doulos** (δοῦλος), v. 1. "Slave, servant." Also an honorific ("servant of the LORD").

**doxa, doxai** (δόξα, δόξαι), vv. 8, 24, 25. "Glory"; in the plural (v. 8), "glorious ones," that is, angels.

**ekporneuō** (ἐκπορνεύω), v. 7. "To give oneself up to sexual immorality." NT hapax.

**ekrizoō** (ἐκριζόω), v. 12. "To uproot."

**eleos, eleaō** (ἔλεος, ἐλεάω), vv. 2, 21, 22, 23. "Mercy"; "to show mercy." In the LXX ἔλεος chiefly translates Hebrew *ḥesed*.

**elenchō** (ἐλέγχω), v. 15. "To expose, convict."

**empaiktēs** (ἐμπαίκτης), v. 18. "Scoffer, mocker." In the NT only here and 2 Pet 3:3.

**enypniazomai** (ἐνυπνιάζομαι), v. 8. "To dream." In the LXX, used of the false prophet as "dreamer" (Deut 13).

**epagōnizomai** (ἐπαγωνίζομαι), v. 3. "To contend for." NT hapax.

**epaphrizō** (ἐπαφρίζω), v. 13. "To foam up." NT hapax.

**epithymia** (ἐπιθυμία), vv. 16, 18. "Desire, craving."

**epoikodomeō** (ἐποικοδομέω), v. 20. "To build upon."

**gongystēs** (γογγυστής), v. 16. "Grumbler, murmurer." NT hapax.

**hagioi** (ἅγιοι), vv. 3, 14. "Holy ones": God's people (v. 3) or angels (v. 14).

**hagiōtatos** (ἁγιώτατος), v. 20. "Most holy." The only superlative of ἅγιος in the NT.

**hapax** (ἅπαξ), vv. 3, 5. "Once, once for all."

**harpazō** (ἁρπάζω), v. 23. "To seize, snatch."

**hyperonkos** (ὑπέρογκος), v. 16. "Swollen, excessive; arrogant (of speech)."

**hypechō** (ὑπέχω), v. 7. "To undergo, suffer (a penalty)." NT hapax.

**katenōpion** (κατενώπιον), v. 24. "Before, in the presence of."

**klētos** (κλητός), v. 1. "Called."

**koinos** (κοινός), v. 3. "Common, shared."

**kratos** (κράτος), v. 25. "Power, dominion." Common in doxologies.

**kyriotēs** (κυριότης), v. 8. "Lordship, dominion."

**megalōsynē** (μεγαλωσύνη), v. 25. "Greatness, majesty."

**mempsimoiros** (μεμψίμοιρος), v. 16. "Complaining of one's lot, querulous." NT hapax.

**metatithēmi** (μετατίθημι), v. 4. "To change, transfer; to turn one thing into a pretext for another."

**miainō** (μιαίνω), v. 8. "To defile."

**oikētērion** (οἰκητήριον), v. 6. "Dwelling, habitation."

**ouai** (οὐαί), v. 11. "Woe!" The prophetic announcement of judgment.

**paradidōmi** (παραδίδωμι), v. 3. "To hand over, deliver." The technical term for passing on tradition.

**parapherō** (παραφέρω), v. 12. "To carry away."

**pareisdyō** (παρεισδύω), v. 4. "To slip in secretly." NT hapax.

**phthinopōrinos** (φθινοπωρινός), v. 12. "Of late autumn." NT hapax.

**phylassō** (φυλάσσω), v. 24. "To guard, protect." Near synonym of τηρέω.

**physikōs** (φυσικῶς), v. 10. "Naturally, by instinct." NT hapax.

**planē** (πλάνη), v. 11. "Error, deception, wandering."

**planētēs** (πλανήτης), v. 13. "Wanderer"; ἀστέρες πλανῆται, "wandering stars." The source of English "planet." NT hapax.

**plēthynō** (πληθύνω), v. 2. "To multiply." πληθυνθείη: "may it be multiplied" (optative).

**poimainō** (ποιμαίνω), v. 12. "To shepherd, tend."

**prographō** (προγράφω), v. 4. "To write beforehand."

**psychikos** (ψυχικός), v. 19. "Natural, soulish, unspiritual."

**sklēros** (σκληρός), v. 15. "Hard, harsh"; "harsh words."

**sōtēr** (σωτήρ), v. 25. "Savior." A title also given to kings and emperors in the Greco-Roman world.

**spilas** (σπιλάς), v. 12. "Reef, rock in the sea"; in later Greek, "stain." NT hapax. See Chapter 6.

**spiloō** (σπιλόω), v. 23. "To stain, defile."

**syneuōcheomai** (συνευωχέομαι), v. 12. "To feast together." In the NT only here and 2 Pet 2:13.

**tēreō** (τηρέω), vv. 1, 6, 13, 21. "To keep, guard, preserve." Jude's key word.

**zophos** (ζόφος), vv. 6, 13. "Deep gloom," associated with the underworld.

## Grammatical and theological terms

**Aorist.** A Greek verb form that presents an action as a whole, without reference to its duration.

**Canon.** The list of books a community recognizes as Scripture.

**Critical text.** A Greek text reconstructed by editors from the manuscripts in order to approximate the earliest recoverable text (for example, NA28, SBLGNT).

**Byzantine text / Majority Text.** The form of the Greek text found in the majority of (mostly later) manuscripts, edited by Robinson and Pierpont among others.

**Doxology.** A formula ascribing glory to God.

**Etymological fallacy.** The mistake of assuming that a word's origin or component parts determine its meaning in use.

**Hapax legomenon** (plural *hapax legomena*). A word that occurs only once in a given body of literature, here the New Testament.

**LXX (Septuagint).** The Greek translation of the Hebrew scriptures, used widely by Greek-speaking Jews and early Christians.

**Optative.** A Greek verb mood used for wishes and prayers. It is rare in the New Testament.

**Participle.** A verbal adjective ("having been loved," "praying").

**Perfect tense.** A Greek tense presenting a completed action with continuing results.

**Pre-existence (of Christ).** The belief that the Son existed before his human birth.

**Prophetic aorist.** The use of a past tense to describe a future event as certain.

**Pseudepigrapha.** Ancient Jewish (and Christian) writings attributed to biblical figures, such as 1 Enoch and the *Testament of Moses*.

**Pseudonymity.** Writing under another person's name.

**Second Temple Judaism.** Judaism from the rebuilding of the temple (late sixth century BC) to its destruction in AD 70.

**Targum.** An Aramaic translation and paraphrase of the Hebrew scriptures.

**Textus Receptus (TR).** The printed Greek text that underlies the King James Version.

**Theophany.** An appearance of God.

**Type / typology.** A person or event in Scripture understood as a pattern of later persons or events.

**Watchers.** In 1 Enoch, the angels who descended to earth and took human wives (Gen 6:1–4).
